\section{Introduction}
Scientific telemetry archives must reconcile sustained ingest rates, bounded reconstruction error, random access, and reproducibility. Lossless byte-oriented compressors preserve every floating-point bit but do not exploit an application's admissible distortion. Error-bounded scientific compressors such as SZ3~\cite{liang2022sz3} and fixed-rate floating-point representations such as ZFP~\cite{lindstrom2014zfp} address complementary portions of this design space. Streaming deployments add a further constraint: an encoder cannot revise previously transmitted blocks when source statistics change.

\Aether{} is a block-streaming codec for one-dimensional, single-precision telemetry. It uses decoder-synchronous closed-loop prediction, generalized-error residual modeling, entropy-constrained quantizer construction, and interleaved rANS coding~\cite{duda2013ans}. Its two operating modes enforce either a serialized rate budget or a pointwise absolute-error bound. An optional CRC-protected index supports bounded-range decoding without scanning the complete stream.

This artifact adds three forms of evidence needed for reproducible evaluation. First, it reports a dimensionally consistent Shannon lower bound together with spectral, arrival-time, and peak-acceleration metrics. Second, it records architectural counters and a Roofline operational point, while explicitly identifying modeled operation counts and permission-restricted fallbacks. Third, it generates vector figures at IEEE column widths directly from measured result files. Earthquake traces retain their FDSN query provenance through EarthScope and USGS-compatible services~\cite{fdsnweb,usgsfdsn}.
